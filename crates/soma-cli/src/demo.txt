1. Semimartingales. Let X be a continuous semimartingale with respect to a filtration satisfying the usual conditions. We write X = M + A where M is a continuous local martingale and A is a process of finite variation.
2. Quadratic variation. The quadratic variation of X is defined as the limit in probability of sums of squared increments over partitions whose mesh tends to zero. For Brownian motion the quadratic variation over the interval from zero to t equals t almost surely, which is the source of every correction term in stochastic calculus.
3. The stochastic integral. For a predictable process H that is square integrable against the quadratic variation, the stochastic integral of H with respect to M is the unique continuous local martingale whose covariation with every other local martingale N equals the integral of H against the covariation of M and N.
4. The Itô isometry. By the Itô isometry the stochastic integral is an isometry between the space of square integrable integrands and the space of square integrable martingales: the expected square of the integral equals the expected integral of the squared integrand against the quadratic variation.
5. Itô's formula. If f is twice continuously differentiable then f of X is again a semimartingale, and its decomposition contains a second order term equal to one half of the second derivative integrated against the quadratic variation. This term has no analogue in ordinary calculus.
6. Change of measure. Girsanov's theorem describes how the drift of a semimartingale changes under an equivalent change of measure. The Radon-Nikodym density process is itself a positive martingale with unit expectation, and Novikov's condition is sufficient for the exponential local martingale to be a true martingale.
7. No arbitrage. The martingale property together with the no-arbitrage condition implies the existence of an equivalent martingale measure under which discounted prices are martingales. Conversely, if such a measure exists, no admissible strategy can produce an arbitrage.
8. Dominated convergence. Dominated convergence lets us exchange limit and expectation when the integrands are bounded by an integrable random variable. This is used repeatedly when passing from simple integrands to general predictable ones.
9. Localization. Many statements hold first for bounded martingales and extend to local martingales by stopping at a localizing sequence of stopping times that increases to infinity. Care is needed because a local martingale need not be a martingale, even when it is uniformly integrable on compact time intervals.
10. Exercises. Show that the quadratic variation of a continuous finite variation process vanishes. Show that the stochastic exponential solves a linear stochastic differential equation. Explain why the Itô isometry fails without predictability.
